## Adversarial review

**What model use is justified**
Only the bounded, source-linked transcription result (Astra-low, one compact MYBPC3 table, 48/48 people, $0.10, 50s) supports a concrete recommendation: use Astra for narrow, schema-fixed reading subtasks. Nothing here justifies replacing the Grok 4.3 primary. Astra-medium as full-paper primary is strictly worse on the headline identity metric (77.2% vs 88.7% recall, precision roughly comparable) at ~9x the returned cost and far higher unknown-usage exposure ($9.82 vs $0 reserve). The independent clinical-reader overlay is not a usable production feature yet: 9 papers triggered, only 1 field accepted across the entire panel, and one paper never even got dispatched because the budget guard blocked it before the call. Calling this an "overlay" a little overstates its current contribution — it is a single accepted carrier count against a $6.41+$8.27 spend. That asymmetry (cost vs the single accepted fact) is the sharpest number in the whole report and should be foregrounded, not left in a caveat paragraph.

**Denominator / causal issues**
Most of the obvious traps are pre-empted in the write-up (abstentions not scored as correct zeros, identity-miss rows excluded from count denominators, raw-lane field counts explicitly barred from mixing with the row-level rejection-reason tally). I did not find an arithmetic error: the without-shortcut and without-preflagged-disputes subsets, the clinical-trigger counts (6+1+1+1=9, dispatched=8), and the $145.04 envelope all reconcile against the underlying JSON. The one place presentation risks overclaiming is the three-column results table: the "Grok 4.3 + Astra clinical reader" column is visually a coequal arm, but its TP/FP/FN are identical to control except for one field — it is really "control plus one fact," not an independent data point. The prose corrects this a few paragraphs later, but a reader skimming only the table could walk away thinking three meaningfully different pipelines were compared at parity. The other soft spot is the post-hoc "without deterministic shortcuts" slice: dropping two large shared-path papers takes Astra's recall from 77% to 47% on an 8-paper remainder — a swing driven by very small per-paper N (several papers contribute only 1–2 rows). It's labeled "descriptive, not a replacement gate," which is the right hedge, but the magnitude of that swing invites over-reading if quoted out of context.

**Does this measure a 75%-of-human ceiling?**
No, and the document says so explicitly. This is correct: there's no blinded human re-annotation here, the papers were previously opened (failure-enriched, not a random or blind sample), and several results are gated by things unrelated to model capability (missing Table 1 body, 403s on republication fetch, an output-budget wall on 32k reasoning tokens, a hard budget guard blocking a dispatch outright). Any of those could independently explain a chunk of the recall gap. The document's refusal to convert this into a ceiling estimate is the correct call, not a hedge to be relaxed later.

**What the next test should isolate**
The current design confounds too many things at once to attribute the Astra shortfall to the model itself: full-paper scope vs bounded-table scope, transcription vs derived-count aggregation, 15k vs 32k output cap, and reasoning effort (low vs medium) all move together between the "wins" and "losses." The next controlled test should hold three of those four constant and vary one: specifically, run Astra at low vs medium effort on the *same* bounded, schema-fixed roster task, with the *same* output cap, to find out whether the full-paper failures are an effort/cap artifact or a genuine capability gap. Only after that should scope be widened back toward full-paper extraction.

**Are the budget/operational caveats honest?**
Yes. The $150 ceiling, CLI exclusion, unreconciled-Azure-invoice caveat, and $25 uncertainty margin are stated plainly rather than folded into a clean-looking total, and the abandoned Grok 4.6 pilots (effort silently dropped by the SDK) are excluded from scoring rather than quietly averaged in. Nothing found here understates cost or overstates capability beyond the table-formatting nuance above.
