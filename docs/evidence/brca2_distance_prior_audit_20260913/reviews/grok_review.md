**A nonzero neighborhood score in an all-unaffected segment is not, by itself, evidence that the kernel is too long-ranged.** With a no-hard-cutoff kernel, target exclusion, and distinct substitutions at the same residue kept eligible, the synthetic identities already separate shrinkage, aggregation grain, and weight normalization from kernel range. Arithmetic: \(\alpha=1,\beta=9\) and \(A=0,U=1\) give \(p=(1+0)/(1+9+1)=1/11\approx 9.09\%\). Any convex combination of identical labels is \(9.09\%\). Pooling the same 100 unaffecteds under one prior is \(1/110\approx 0.909\%\), a different estimand that uses all observations and is not a held-out prediction. An own posterior \(\le 0.1\%\) with \(A=0\) needs \(1/(10+U)\le 0.001\), hence \(U\ge 990\). No cases plus sparse exposure is not strong evidence of very low risk.

**1. Convex combination, no universal floor, what an all-zero segment isolates.**
\(D_i=\sum_j W_{ij}p_j\) with \(\sum_j W_{ij}=1\), \(W_{ij}\ge 0\), so \(\min_j p_j\le D_i\le\max_j p_j\). That constraint is not a universal positive floor on all count configurations: \(A=0\) and large \(U\) send \(p_j\to 0\); nonzero \(A\) send \(p_j\) upward. The synthetic \(\sim 9\%\) level is the shared Beta mean \(\alpha/(\alpha+\beta)=0.1\) after one unaffected per variant, not a kernel artifact. Kernel range, implementation, renormalization of tiny raw \(K\), shared per-variant shrinkage, and variant-versus-carrier aggregation are distinct. Range only matters when donor \(p_j\) (or \(n_j\)) vary with distance. Renormalization can promote far donors when near support is empty; that is a support problem, not proof of a too-long tail. Shrinkage is applied once per variant before averaging, so sparse variants remain pinned near the prior. Averaging variant-level \(p_j\) treats each substitution as one unit, unlike pooling carriers. An eligible segment with every \(A_j=0\) and small \(U_j\) isolates the **prior-dominated variant-level surface**. It does not isolate “risk is \(0.1\%\)” and does not, by geometry alone, imply a kernel defect. Unaligned Euclidean distances across coordinate frames remain invalid; a generic \(b\sqrt{\text{sequence separation}}\) is a separate frame, not a substitute for 3-D distance.

**2. Three estimands.**
- \(\sum W p\): smoothed mean of already-shrunk variant posteriors. Transfer is of **risk estimates**, not of counts. Positive scaling of raw \(K\) cancels in \(W\).
- \(\sum W(A/n)\): same grain without shrinkage; undefined or unstable at \(n=0\) or \(n=1\).
- Kernel-pooled \((\alpha+\sum K A)/(\alpha+\beta+\sum K n)\): **one** prior on kernel-weighted counts. Transfer is of observations, i.e. approximate exchangeability after \(K\). Raw-\(K\) scale changes effective sample size; it is not invariant.

Normalized-\(W\) pooling of \(p_j\) therefore cannot be “fixed” by rescaling \(K\), and it does not accumulate exposure the way the pooled estimator does. Averaging \(D\) across coordinate contexts can count the same \(A,U\) more than once if contexts are then treated as independent evidence. Regional evidence is transferable to a held-out variant only under a similarity hypothesis (shared mechanism, comparable ascertainment). That is not a mathematical consequence of \(W\) summing to 1. Target exclusion correctly keeps \(D_i\) a neighborhood feature rather than the variant’s own posterior; same-residue alleles may share structure and still be distinct binomials.

**3. Support-aware diagnostics, primary estimator unchanged.**
Retain the positive-tail kernel. Report, at least: absolute kernel mass \(\sum K\) before normalization; distance to the nearest eligible donor; radius holding a stated share of \(\sum W\) (e.g. 50%, 90%); effective donor number \(1/\sum W^2\); kernel-weighted and unweighted \(A\) and \(U\); a dispersion or interval among the \(p_j\) (point \(D_i\) hides that all mass may sit on weakly identified labels); and disagreement of \(D\) across frames. Far-tail decay of \(K_h(d)=2/(1+\exp(\log 3\cdot d/h))\) does not imply negligible **normalized** influence: if all eligible mass is in the tail, \(W\) still sums to 1. Abstention (too little absolute mass, no nearby donor) or a switch to pooled counts or a different prior must be named and justified. Silently substituting another estimator to force \(0.1\%\) is not a diagnostic.

**4. Source-count audit (general).**
Catalogue membership, pathogenicity class, and observed carrier counts are different data types. Counts-based \(p_j\) first need observation ownership (who is attached to which allele), a defined affected endpoint, and deduplication across studies, relatives, and overlapping cohorts. Population carriers are taken as unaffected by the stated modeling assumption; that still requires that those records are counts, not labels. No specific source error is asserted or confirmed here.

**5. Order of operations.**
(i) Treat the convex-combination and \(1/11\) vs \(1/110\) vs \(U\ge 990\) identities as deductions, independent of any real file. (ii) Audit counts before interpreting any \(D\). (iii) Compute support diagnostics without retuning \(h\) or \((\alpha,\beta)\) to a target risk. (iv) Compare the three estimands as different questions. (v) Evaluate transfer with held-out, well-counted variants and calibration where \(n_j\) is large—not by flattening a homogeneous prior-dominated segment. A narrower kernel cannot fix a curve that is flat because every eligible \(p_j\) is the same. Zero observed cases do not establish \(0.1\%\) risk. Whether neighborhoods are biologically exchangeable, and whether real \(A_j,U_j\) are valid, are hypotheses for data; they are not settled by this conceptual exercise, which is not a verification of any dataset.
