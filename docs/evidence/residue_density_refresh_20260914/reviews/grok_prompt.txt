PROPOSED FRAMEWORK AND FIXED CHOICES
For each gene and variant class separately (here missense), fit an empirical Beta prior. A is observed affected-carrier count and U is observed unaffected count; population-reference carriers are treated as unaffected by an explicit modeling assumption. Accept that assumption for this review instead of disputing it. For each variant unit j, n_j=A_j+U_j and p_j=(alpha+A_j)/(alpha+beta+n_j). Alpha corresponds to affected and beta to unaffected. A descriptive neighborhood feature D_i is sum_j W_ij*p_j with nonnegative normalized geometry weights and the target variant unit excluded; distinct variants at the same residue remain eligible. This feature is shown alongside a variant's own posterior; neither has independent clinical calibration just by construction.

The historical fitting formula is y=A/n, w=1-1/(n+0.01), mu=sum(w*y)/sum(w), v=sum(w*(y-mu)^2)/M, M=number of eligible units, then kappa=mu*(1-mu)/v-1, alpha=mu*kappa and beta=(1-mu)*kappa. This is the stated historical weighted mean/MSE convention, not normalized weighted variance or MAE. The prior remains fixed for the descriptive target-excluded feature. Do not silently change that convention or call this independent prior-held-out validation.

The no-hard-cutoff kernel is K(d)=2/(1+exp(log(3)*d/3)), with distances in Angstrom. It is 1 at 0 and 1/2 at 3 Angstrom. Same-IDR-segment sequence pairs use d=3.8*sqrt(abs(residue_i-residue_j)); separate local coordinate frames do not share unaligned Euclidean distances. Normalize weights within each eligible context; if multiple supported contexts are averaged equally, show this choice and its support dependence. Positive log-space tails may remain eligible even with small raw kernel mass. Preserve missing geometry and unsupported positions as missing, not numerical zero. Do not replace the requested primary with a hard cutoff.

Plot a separate residue panel per gene with the primary neighborhood posterior mean and donor/geometry coverage. Multiple distinct variant units can map to one residue and can have different target-excluded scores. A plotted residue summary must declare its aggregation, weighting and multiplicity. Distinguish experimental/local predicted 3D geometry, polymer estimates, and unsupported residues. Long gaps must not be falsely bridged by line interpolation.

SYNTHETIC ARITHMETIC AND DIAGNOSTICS
For a synthetic Beta(1,9) prior, each A=0/U=1 variant has p=1/11=9.09%; a normalized average of identical such labels remains 9.09%, however narrow the weights. Pooling 100 such unaffected observations with one prior gives 1/110=0.909%; this is not a held-out prediction. Under that synthetic prior A=0 needs U>=990 to reach an own posterior <=0.1%. No such numerical risk claim is being made about a real gene.
Compare the meanings of primary sum(W*p), a diagnostic raw-fraction neighborhood sum(W*A/n), and a diagnostic one-prior kernel pool (alpha+sum(K*A))/(alpha+beta+sum(K*n)). These differ in estimand, carrier/variant weighting, prior application and sensitivity to the absolute scale of K. A normalized-W pool is not the same as a raw-K pool. Reusing observations across frames must not silently multiply count exposure or confidence. A small empirical fraction is not automatically a calibrated disease probability; an all-zero-affected neighborhood with little U need not approach 0.1% under this model.
