# Methodological review of a residue-neighborhood plotting framework

This is a design review of a stated procedure, not a validation of any gene, variant class, source catalogue, geometry, fitted prior, or implementation. All numerical examples below are synthetic illustrations of arithmetic and estimands. The review accepts, as instructed, the modeling choice that population-reference carriers are treated as unaffected.

The framework is a descriptive smoother: for missense units, an empirical Beta prior is fit once, each unit receives a posterior mean \(p_j=(\alpha+A_j)/(\alpha+\beta+n_j)\), and a neighborhood feature \(D_i=\sum_j W_{ij}p_j\) uses nonnegative, normalized, target-excluded geometry weights. Neither \(p_j\) nor \(D_i\) is a calibrated disease probability by construction. That distinction is the first thing plots and claims must not erase.

## 1. Fitting, orientation, units, and mapping

The historical fit is a weighted mean/MSE convention, not a proper weighted variance and not MAE. With \(y=A/n\), \(w=1-1/(n+0.01)\), \(\mu=\sum(w y)/\sum w\), \(v=\sum w(y-\mu)^2/M\), \(\kappa=\mu(1-\mu)/v-1\), \(\alpha=\mu\kappa\), \(\beta=(1-\mu)\kappa\), the divisor is \(M\) (eligible-unit count), not \(\sum w\) or \(\sum w-1\). That is an intentional convention, not a silent bug. It is still a fragile estimator: one or two high-\(n\) outliers, a mass of \(n=1\) units, or a near-0/1 \(\mu\) can drive \(v\) to 0 or \(\kappa\) negative. Falsifiable checks, independent of “the code ran”: (i) if all eligible \(y\) are identical, \(v=0\) and \(\kappa\) is undefined—the procedure must fail loudly, not emit a huge \(\kappa\); (ii) swapping the meaning of \(A\) and \(U\) must reverse \(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) and map a synthetic all-unaffected unit from \(p\approx\mu\) toward 0, not toward 1; (iii) adding a duplicate of every unit should not be treated as new evidence for \(\mu\) unless multiplicity is an explicit, defended choice.

Target exclusion is a modeling convention, not validation. The prior is fit on the full eligible set and then frozen while \(D_i\) omits only the target unit. That is not prior-held-out evaluation. A unit with large \(n_j\) can still dominate \(\alpha,\beta\) and then appear “independent” of its neighborhood. Same-residue alternatives remain eligible: two missense events at residue \(R\) can each borrow the other’s \(p_j\). That is intentional. A residue-level plot that then averages those \(D_i\) without declaring multiplicity will look like independent local confirmation. Required declaration: aggregation (mean / n-weighted mean / max), weighting, and how many distinct variant units contributed.

Coordinate and isoform errors are implementation failures, not conventions. Sequence distance \(3.8\sqrt{|\Delta \mathrm{res}|}\) is only for same-IDR-segment pairs; unaligned local frames must not share Euclidean distances. Falsifiers: (a) a pair with no shared supported context still receives a finite \(d\); (b) an isoform offset of \(k\) residues systematically shifts neighbors by \(k\); (c) a chain/model mismatch assigns polymer C\(\alpha\) distances as if they were experimental 3D; (d) missing geometry is written as weight 0 rather than missing, so “no structure” plots as “no neighborhood risk.” Preserve missing as missing.

## 2. Flat surfaces: shrinkage, composition, and kernel range

A normalized average of identical shrunk labels is that label. Under a synthetic Beta(1,9) prior, every \(A=0,U=1\) unit has \(p=1/11\approx 9.09\%\). Any \(W\)-normalized mix of such units remains \(9.09\%\), however local the kernel. That is repeated sparse-variant shrinkage, not proof of a long-range biological field. Pooling the same 100 units into one Beta update, \((\alpha+\sum A)/(\alpha+\beta+\sum n)=1/110\approx 0.909\%\), is a different estimand: one-prior kernel pooling, not \(D_i\). It is also not held-out prediction. Under that same synthetic prior, an isolated \(A=0\) unit needs \(U\ge 990\) before its own posterior is \(\le 0.1\%\). No such numerical claim is being made about a real gene; the arithmetic only shows that “all nearby labels are 0” does not imply a 0.1% neighborhood under this model if \(U\) is small.

Three quantities must not be used interchangeably:

| Quantity | Estimand | Prior | Kernel scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary \(\sum W p_j\) | mean of already-shrunk unit posteriors | applied per unit, then averaged | invariant to positive rescaling of \(K\) after normalization |
| Raw-fraction \(\sum W(A_j/n_j)\) | mean of MLE fractions | none in the average | likewise invariant after normalization |
| One-prior pool \((\alpha+\sum K A)/(\alpha+\beta+\sum K n)\) | single posterior as if kernel-weighted counts were one sample | applied once to pooled counts | sensitive to absolute \(K\) mass |

A normalized-\(W\) pool is not a raw-\(K\) pool. Reusing the same carriers across overlapping frames, isoforms, or polymer copies must not multiply count exposure. If several supported contexts are averaged equally, that choice and its support dependence belong on the figure; equal averaging of a well-supported 3D context and a barely supported polymer context is a different feature from a mass-weighted mix.

Steep raw tail decay does not imply negligible normalized distant influence. If local donors are absent, remaining mass on far residues is renormalized to 1. Distinguishing diagnostics, all measurable without claiming calibration: raw kernel mass \(\sum K\); distance to nearest eligible donor; radii containing 50% and 90% of normalized \(W\); effective donor count \((\sum W)^2/\sum W^2\); contribution decomposition (which units supply 80% of \(D_i\)); disagreement across contexts; and bandwidth sensitivity (repeat at half and double the 3 Å half-mass scale). A surface that stays flat while nearest-donor distance and 90% \(W\)-radius both explode is composition-plus-shrinkage. A surface that stays high only when a few high-\(p\) donors remain inside a short \(W\)-radius is local. A surface that moves when \(K\) is rescaled before normalization is a pooling bug, because the primary feature should be scale-invariant after \(W\)-normalization.

Positive log-space tails may keep distant units eligible even when raw \(K\) is tiny; after normalization those tails are not tiny. Do not replace the requested no-hard-cutoff kernel \(K(d)=2/(1+e^{\log 3\cdot d/3})\) with a cutoff and then argue the cutoff was “equivalent.”

## 3. Biological reasonableness without causal overclaim

A neighborhood of missense posterior means is not a statement that every substitution at a “hot” residue causes disease. Tolerated and disruptive substitutions can co-locate; loss-of-function and gain-of-function can point opposite directions; germline and somatic records are different sampling processes; age, sex, time horizon, and ascertainment differ across catalogues. Opposite phenotype directions averaged into one \(A,U\) pair are not a mixture model—they are cancellation. A catalogue classification (P/LP, VUS, “pathogenic in ClinVar”) is not an observed patient count, not an endpoint, and not \(A\). Treating classifications as counts silently converts curator opinion into a Beta observation.

The accepted assumption that population-reference carriers are unaffected is still an assumption: it can be approximately right for highly penetrant, early-onset disease and badly wrong for late-onset, incomplete-penetrance, or poorly phenotyped traits. The review does not dispute it here, but any risk language that follows from \(U\) being “unaffected” inherits that limit. Source rows need correct variant class, gene, and genotype typing; repeated people and overlapping cohorts need deduplication. Distinct relatives are observations but are dependent; the Beta count model has no kinship term. Internal agreement between \(D_i\) and the same fitted \(p_j\) is circular. It cannot establish disease-risk calibration.

## 4. Plot semantics and pass/fail checks

Own posterior \(p_j\) and neighborhood feature \(D_i\) must be visually and verbally distinct. Residues are not variant units: several units can map to one residue with different target-excluded scores. Unsupported geometry is not zero neighborhood. Polymer estimates are not experimental or local predicted 3D. Between-variant spread is not a posterior interval; this model does not supply a calibrated uncertainty band around \(D_i\), and none should be invented. Count-weighted residue summaries answer a different question from equal-variant summaries; whichever is drawn must be named.

Practical pass/fail checks: (1) a residue with no supported context is omitted or explicitly missing, not plotted at 0; (2) long sequence gaps are broken, not linearly interpolated across unsupported residues; (3) experimental/local 3D, polymer, and unsupported positions use distinct marks; (4) a residue that hosts \(m>1\) units shows \(m\) and the aggregation rule; (5) the primary trace is \(D_i=\sum W p_j\), with raw-fraction and one-prior-pool traces labeled diagnostics if shown; (6) donor/geometry coverage is plotted, not only the mean; (7) no interval is drawn unless a stated variance estimator is introduced and stress-tested. Fail any of these and the figure is not a faithful rendering of the model.

## 5. What should happen before any risk-target tuning

Priority is source ownership and endpoint validation, then independent evaluation, then—if still justified—threshold tuning. Confirm that each \(A\) and \(U\) is a person-level observation with a defined phenotype window, not a reused classification row. Deduplicate repeated submissions and cohort overlaps. Keep genuine relatives but report dependence. External checks that can falsify parts of the pipeline: orthogonal functional assays (can challenge “this substitution is disruptive,” cannot calibrate lifetime risk); case–control or prospective cohorts with the same endpoint and ancestry structure (can challenge calibration of \(p_j\) or \(D_i\), cannot be replaced by another pass through the fitted labels); inter-lab geometry concordance (can challenge coordinate mapping, cannot validate the Beta). Catalogue-internal replication of the same posterior labels is not independent evaluation.

Until those checks exist, keep alternatives labeled diagnostics. Do not treat a small empirical neighborhood fraction as a calibrated probability, and do not claim that an all-zero-affected neighborhood with little \(U\) approaches 0.1% under this model. The honest product of the framework is a geometry-weighted, prior-shrunk, target-excluded descriptive feature with explicit missingness—not a clinical risk score.
