Here's the adversarial pass, organized by the five areas requested.

**Causal claims**
The headline "Grok 4.6 is working again" oversells what a handful of successful calls demonstrate. The body admits "cap not sole cause," "Azure internal root cause unknown," and "no claim fix cures availability" — so no mechanism was actually identified for the fix. What *was* verified (the `max_completion_tokens` cap-name translation, wire payload, live SDK call) is a distinct bug from the timeout/availability incident being narrated. Conflating "we fixed a parameter-naming bug" with "the outage is resolved" lets the title claim more than the counterbalanced 3-call latency check (1.86/2.49/2.09s) supports. This is an n=3 confirmatory sample plus 8 packet calls — thin grounds for asserting a production-relevant state change rather than "it worked this time."

**Denominators**
The report juggles 216/216, 168/168, 141, 122, 93, and 56 — each legitimate for a narrow question but a minefield for selective quotation. The primary table leads with 216/216 (duplicate-inflated) before the dedup explanation; a skimming reader anchors on the bigger, cleaner number. More concerning: the 141-row "strict source citation" denominator lumps incommensurate failure types across arms as if comparable. Astra's 15 failures (141→126) are entirely one packet's *cosmetic* citation decoration (`Table 1, L107` vs `L107`). Grok's 50 failures (141→91) are 28 null-vs-false value-flag mismatches plus 22 missing enrollment citations — categorically different (one is a schema/value ambiguity, not a citation failure at all). Presenting "126/141" vs "91/141" side-by-side implies a ranked accuracy comparison on the same construct, when the two arms are actually failing at different things for different reasons. That should be split into separate metrics, not blended into one gate.

**Source/gold acceptance**
The disclaimers (investigator-adjudicated, not blind validation; not a general production validator) are appropriately placed. But the gold-overlap table (43/49 vs baseline 13/49, etc.) is positioned right after the primary result and reads as "new method beats existing gold 3x," despite explicit text disclaiming this. If baseline is genuinely that inaccurate on this narrow RYR2/MYBPC3 overlap, that's a serious claim about the *existing* canonical gold's quality that deserves its own scrutiny, not a paragraph buried under "additional exact null fills." Recommend moving this comparison to an appendix or a separately reviewed section so it can't be cited as evidence for the routing recommendation, which it currently seems to feed rhetorically even though the text says it shouldn't.

**Model routing recommendation**
Recommending Astra-low over medium, and floating Grok as a "cheaper second opinion," rests on 8 packets. The entire Astra low-vs-medium equivalence claim is driven by both efforts failing identically on the *same* one packet (RYR2 citation decoration) — that's one shared failure mode, not evidence of general equivalence across citation styles or paper types. For Grok, the report simultaneously says "no claim fix cures availability" and "use it... once availability is acceptable" — a soft contradiction that still nudges toward adoption before the underlying intermittency is understood. A production routing recommendation shouldn't rest on a single-digit sample with an unresolved backend cause.

**Budget**
The ledger claims five-decimal precision for prior accounting ($145.03751) but then reconciles new spend with "about 1.2 plus failed reserves => under 3" — mismatched precision that hides whether the guard actually fits. It should show exact arithmetic (stated per-arm costs sum to $1.059, not "about 1.2") rather than rounding at the one point that matters for the guard. Separately, proxy pricing is flagged as non-authoritative ("not Azure invoice"), yet the routing section still cites it as a hard 5x cost ratio to justify Grok — pick one: either the pricing is reliable enough to argue routing, or it isn't reliable enough to cite as a ratio.
